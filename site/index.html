Once they’re running

Then it keeps every agent in reach.

Reflex never wraps your agents in an SDK or a chat API. They run in their own TUIs inside herdr, on the plans you already pay for. Reflex just knows where they are.

◐The edge of your screen

A small tab grows from the right edge while agents work: a ring for each, amber when one waits on you, green when done. Hover it for the rail.

▯Take it with you

↺Nothing is lost

Earlier Claude, pi and Codex sessions come back with a keystroke. Background sessions and shells count as work until they’re really done.

☕Awake while they work

Your Mac doesn’t idle-sleep while an agent is working, and lets go on battery before it runs low.

Under the hood

A System 1 model, not a chat agent at the helm.

Reflex asks Jev typed questions over candidates it built itself, and gets back keys and probabilities in about a second.

Candidates, built deterministically

Your live sessions (folder, state, last lines), the git repos under your roots and their parents, and your earlier conversations, ranked so Jev only ever chooses among a few.

One typed call

Which session? Which folder? A side question? Earlier work? Plus where your last requests went, so “also add tests for that” lands right.

Sure, or it asks

When Jev is sure, your words go. When it isn’t, its guess is highlighted for you to confirm. Those rules are laws, machine-checked in Bend.

Get started

Three steps, about five minutes.

Reflex sits on top of what you already run. The first launch checks each piece for you, and everything it checks lives in Settings → Setup afterwards.

01

Run your agents in herdr

herdr 0.9 or later, with its integrations, lets Reflex see each agent’s state and questions. Reflex’s first launch installs both with a click; or do it by hand:

curl -fsSL https://herdr.dev/install.sh | sh herdr integration install claude herdr integration install pi
02

Open Reflex

It walks you through herdr, connecting Jev (and trying a request without sending anything), the folders to index, ⌥⌥ and the edge.

Questions

Good to know.

What leaves my Mac?

Only the routing call. When you ask, Jev gets your request and a short description of the candidates Reflex built: folder names and descriptions, recent commit subjects, each live agent’s last few lines, and the titles of earlier conversations. Never your files or your diffs.

Without a Jev key, nothing leaves at all: you pick where each request goes. Your agents talk to their own providers exactly as they do today.

Does it change what I typed?

No. Reflex decides where and when; your words are delivered verbatim, images included. No model rewrites them.

What if Jev picks the wrong folder?

When Jev isn’t sure, nothing is sent on its own: its guess is highlighted and you confirm or choose another with ↓ ⏎. Reflex remembers what you chose, so a similar request goes there next time. Settings → Routing shows that memory and forgets it.

Which agents work with it?

Anything herdr runs: Claude Code, Codex and pi are first-class, with their earlier sessions resumable and their questions answerable as cards. Your agents stay in their own terminal UIs, on the plans you already pay for.

How does the phone connect?

Over Tailscale, so it is private and works from anywhere. ⋯ on the rail shows a QR code that works once and expires in ten minutes; sign phones out from Settings → Phone. No app to install, and no account of ours.

What does it cost?

Reflex is free and MIT licensed. Routing calls go to your own Jev provider with your own key; everything else runs on your Mac.

Intel Macs, or older macOS?

Not for now: Reflex needs macOS 26 on Apple Silicon.
